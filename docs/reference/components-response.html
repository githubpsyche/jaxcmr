A response distribution gives the probability of STOP followed by each item.

In ordinary CMR the model weights each item by its context match. A semantic model can add the last recalled item’s semantic associations. A positional model can sum the weights of several study positions for the same item. PositionalStop and RecallUntilExhausted use the weights supplied by the model.

Other rules compete STOP directly against raw evidence. They require a model that supplies evidence in those units. LearnedStopCompetition also requires the model to encode a terminal item during study.

Choose among weighted candidates

RecallUntilExhausted Choose among available items in proportion to their model-supplied weights.
PositionalStop Choose STOP by output position and divide the remaining probability among items.
SupportsCandidateWeights Return item weights for a selection of retrieval candidates.
SupportsWeightsAndRecallCount Supply candidate weights and the number of recalls already made.
relative_choice_weights Weight each candidate by its evidence relative to the strongest candidate.
pool_position_weights Sum position weights by recorded item code.

Compete a scheduled STOP with the items

ExponentialStopCompetition Compete exp(stop_log_weight + stop_weight_growth * k) against exp(item evidence).
PowerLawStopCompetition Compete exp(stop_log_weight) * (1 + k)**stop_weight_growth against exp(item evidence).
SupportsItemEvidence Supply item evidence in the model’s units before the item-choice transform.
SupportsEvidenceAndRecallCount Supply item evidence, availability and the number of recalls already made.

Compare exponential evidence for available and unavailable items

ExponentialEvidenceSupportRatio Use exp(item evidence) for both the support ratio and conditional item choice.
SupportsStudiedEvidence Supply evidence for both available and unavailable studied items.

Retrieve a learned terminal item

LearnedStopCompetition Choose among a learned terminal item and eligible study items in proportion to weight.
SupportsTerminalItem Supply raw evidence for the learned terminal candidate followed by the items.

API entries

RecallUntilExhausted

jaxcmr.components.response.RecallUntilExhausted(list_length, parameters)

Choose among available items in proportion to their model-supplied weights.

The policy passes choice_sensitivity to the model. STOP has probability zero while an active trial has an available item, and one otherwise.

PositionalStop

jaxcmr.components.response.PositionalStop(list_length, parameters)

Choose STOP by output position and divide the remaining probability among items.

After k recalls, the scheduled stopping probability is stop_probability_scale * exp(k * stop_probability_growth), subject to the positional probability ceiling. Available items share the remaining probability in proportion to the model’s candidate weights at choice_sensitivity.

SupportsCandidateWeights

jaxcmr.components.response.SupportsCandidateWeights: Protocol

Return item weights for a selection of retrieval candidates.

The mask indexes the same candidates as recallable. Results are in recorded item-code order. Unstudied candidates contribute zero weight.

ExponentialStopCompetition

jaxcmr.components.response.ExponentialStopCompetition(list_length, parameters)

Compete exp(stop_log_weight + stop_weight_growth * k) against exp(item evidence).

k counts previous recalls. The intercept is the log weight of STOP before the first recall. No maximum normalization or CMR choice exponent is applied.

PowerLawStopCompetition

jaxcmr.components.response.PowerLawStopCompetition(list_length, parameters)

Compete exp(stop_log_weight) * (1 + k)**stop_weight_growth against exp(item evidence).

The first response uses k = 0. Item evidence uses the same units as in exponential competition.

ExponentialEvidenceSupportRatio

jaxcmr.components.response.ExponentialEvidenceSupportRatio(list_length, parameters)

Use exp(item evidence) for both the support ratio and conditional item choice.

The numerator sums weights of available studied items. The denominator sums weights of unavailable studied items. Before any item becomes unavailable, STOP has probability stop_probability_scale (subject to the usual cap).

LearnedStopCompetition

jaxcmr.components.response.LearnedStopCompetition(list_length, parameters)

Choose among a learned terminal item and eligible study items in proportion to weight.

The model encodes the terminal item at list end. While retrieval remains possible, the CRU hybrid reserves at least FLOAT32_EPS mass per available item in total. Exhausted or inactive trials stop with probability one.

Use the same factory argument to compare two response rules on ordinary CMR:

from jaxcmr.components.response import PowerLawStopCompetition
from jaxcmr.components.support_ratio import RawSupportRatioSoftmaxChoice
from jaxcmr.models import cmr, terminal_item_cmr

ratio_factory = cmr.make_factory(response_policy_create_fn=RawSupportRatioSoftmaxChoice)
competition_factory = cmr.make_factory(response_policy_create_fn=PowerLawStopCompetition)
terminal_factory = terminal_item_cmr.make_factory()

The third factory adds an end-of-list association during study. To compare response rules while holding that encoding fixed, pass another response_policy_create_fn to terminal_item_cmr.make_factory.

Which models support each rule?

Response rule Models that supply its inputs
Scheduled stopping or recall until exhaustion All configurable CMR variants. Semantic and emotional models provide their own item weights. Positional models sum weights over occurrences of each item.
Raw support-ratio policies, exponential or power-law STOP competition, exponential-evidence support ratio, or the finite-window race Ordinary CMR, CMR without reinstatement, outlist CMR, eCMR (including additive semantic and source-switch eCMR), CMR3 and its source-switch variant, and terminal-item CMR expose raw evidence. The additive semantic retrieval-bias variant requires a policy using candidate weights because its bias operates after the choice transform. Blended positional CMR supplies evidence separately for each route.
Competition with a learned terminal item Terminal-item CMR encodes an end-of-list item and supplies its evidence alongside the study items.

Historical fits can select RelativePowerSupportRatio. It computes candidate weights separately for available and unavailable items, then divides their totals. Use that policy to reproduce fits made with separately weighted groups.

Dual-cue CMR stops when it exhausts its candidates and has a fixed response rule.
